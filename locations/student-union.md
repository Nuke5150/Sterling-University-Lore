# Sterling University Student Union

## Purpose

The Student Union is the social heart of Sterling University. Nearly every student passes through it at some point during the day, whether to eat, study, meet friends, attend club meetings, or simply wait between classes. It is one of the easiest places on campus for characters to naturally cross paths.

---

## Atmosphere

The building is active from morning until late evening.

Conversation forms a constant background hum rather than a distracting noise.

The smell of fresh coffee drifts from the café throughout the day.

Students come and go continuously, carrying backpacks, laptops, and drinks.

Large windows overlook the central quad, making the building feel connected to the rest of campus.

Comfortable seating encourages people to stay longer than they intended.

During Orientation Week the building becomes noticeably more crowded as new students search for classrooms, clubs, and familiar faces.

Despite the activity, the Student Union feels welcoming rather than chaotic.

---

## Layout

### Main Commons

An open seating area with tables of different sizes.

Students eat meals, study, work on assignments, or simply relax between classes.

Finding an empty table during lunch can be difficult.

---

### Campus Café

The most consistently busy space inside the Student Union.

Students stop here before morning classes, between lectures, and late in the evening.

Lines grow longer before 9:00 AM and again around lunchtime.

---

### Club Hallway

Student organizations frequently set up tables here to recruit new members, advertise upcoming events, or raise money.

Flyers, posters, and bulletin boards change throughout the semester.

---

### Study Lounge

A quieter section with comfortable seating, charging stations, and large windows.

Students usually lower their voices here, although whispered conversations still happen.

---

### Meeting Rooms

Reserved by clubs, student government, tutoring groups, and project teams.

Characters may pass by open doors and overhear fragments of conversations without learning complete information.

---

## Daily Life

The Student Union is rarely empty.

Students meet before class.

Friends gather for lunch.

Study groups form and dissolve.

Club members recruit new students.

Professors occasionally stop for coffee.

Campus staff walk through the building.

People search for available outlets to charge laptops.

Someone is almost always looking for a friend who said, "I'm already here."

---

## Story Opportunities

The Student Union naturally supports scenes involving:

- First meetings
- Chance encounters
- Running into classmates unexpectedly
- Lunch conversations
- Club recruitment
- Study sessions
- Group projects
- Awkward introductions
- Brief conversations between classes
- Waiting out bad weather
- Overhearing partial conversations
- Meeting someone through a mutual friend

The location works best for everyday interactions rather than dramatic confrontations.

---

## Background Details

To make scenes feel alive, occasionally include small background details such as:

- A campus tour walking through.
- A barista calling out drink orders.
- Someone dropping a stack of books.
- Club members handing out flyers.
- Students searching for open seats.
- Phones buzzing with campus alerts.
- Rain tapping against the windows.
- A maintenance worker replacing a ceiling light.
- Friends greeting one another as they pass.

Background activity should support the atmosphere without distracting from the main scene.

---

## Continuity Rules

The Student Union should almost never feel empty.

Lunch hours are the busiest time of day.

Orientation Week significantly increases foot traffic.

The café is consistently popular.

## What This Location Is Not

The Student Union is not a place where every visit becomes a major story event.

It is an everyday space.

Characters often stop here for ordinary reasons, and many visits should be brief or uneventful.

The building should feel familiar through repeated use rather than constantly introducing new surprises.

Characters from different majors and social circles naturally cross paths here.

Not every visit needs to become an important scene. Sometimes characters simply eat lunch, study quietly, or pass through on their way somewhere else.
