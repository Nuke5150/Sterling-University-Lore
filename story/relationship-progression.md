# Relationship Progression

## Purpose

Relationships at Sterling University develop gradually through repeated interactions, shared experiences, trust, and time.

Friendships, rivalries, romances, and professional relationships are built over many conversations rather than a single meaningful moment.

No relationship should feel predetermined.

---

# Core Principles

People rarely reveal their full personalities during first meetings.

First impressions are often incomplete or incorrect.

Trust is earned through consistency rather than dramatic gestures.

Respect usually develops before friendship.

Friendship usually develops before vulnerability.

Romantic attraction, when it develops, grows naturally rather than appearing immediately.

Misunderstandings are normal.

Changing someone's opinion of another person takes time.

---

# First Meetings

Early conversations remain focused on immediate situations.

Characters are usually polite but cautious.

Most people reveal very little about themselves.

Conversations often revolve around:

- classes
- campus life
- assignments
- finding locations
- shared circumstances
- observations
- practical problems

Characters should not immediately understand one another.

Avoid deep emotional conversations.

Avoid instant chemistry.

Avoid instant dislike unless justified by behavior.

---

# Acquaintances

After several interactions:

Characters begin recognizing each other around campus.

They remember previous conversations.

Small inside jokes may begin to appear.

Conversations become slightly more relaxed.

Trust is still limited.

People continue learning about one another.

---

# Friends

Friendships develop through repeated positive experiences.

Friends begin volunteering information without being asked.

Conversations wander naturally between topics.

Silences become comfortable.

Teasing becomes more personal but remains good-natured.

Characters naturally check on one another.

Disagreements do not immediately threaten the friendship.

---

# Close Friends

Close friends understand each other's habits and personalities.

They notice when something feels different.

Conversations require less explanation.

Shared history naturally appears in dialogue.

Comfort replaces formality.

Characters may disagree honestly because trust already exists.

---

# Romance

Romantic relationships should never feel rushed.

Physical attraction alone is not enough to build a relationship.

Mutual respect, trust, curiosity, and emotional compatibility should develop first.

Early attraction is often subtle.

Characters rarely recognize their own feelings immediately.

Relationships should evolve naturally through repeated interactions.

Romance should emerge from the story rather than dominate it.

---

# Conflict

Most conflicts begin through:

- misunderstandings
- different priorities
- personality differences
- conflicting goals
- poor timing
- stress

Avoid creating conflict simply for drama.

Characters should have understandable reasons for their actions.

People are rarely entirely right or entirely wrong.

---

# Reconciliation

Trust is rebuilt slowly.

Apologies matter most when followed by changed behavior.

One conversation rarely fixes a damaged relationship.

Characters remember meaningful conflicts.

Forgiveness is earned rather than assumed.

---

# Social Groups

Established friend groups have shared routines and inside jokes.

New students are not immediately included.

People naturally introduce new acquaintances gradually.

Existing relationships continue even when Blake is not present.

The campus does not revolve around one person.

---

# Character Growth

People change slowly.

Experiences influence future behavior.

Characters learn from repeated successes and failures.

Growth should feel earned rather than sudden.

Flaws are not removed overnight.

Relationships should change both people involved.

---

# Story Philosophy

Meaningful relationships are built through many ordinary conversations.

Quiet moments often matter more than dramatic ones.

Characters should surprise one another gradually as trust develops.

Not every relationship becomes a friendship.

Not every friendship becomes romance.

Some people remain acquaintances.

Some people become rivals.

Every relationship should feel unique.

---

# What To Avoid

Avoid instant best friends.

Avoid love at first sight.

Avoid immediate emotional vulnerability.

Avoid characters completely understanding each other after one conversation.

Avoid forcing romance.

Avoid making every disagreement become a major conflict.



Avoid making every interaction emotionally significant.

Ordinary conversations are the foundation of meaningful relationships.

# Existing Relationships

Most students have known each other for months.

Many friendships, rivalries, routines, and reputations already exist before Blake arrives.

Characters naturally reference previous events, classes, professors, campus traditions, and shared experiences.

Blake enters an existing social world rather than creating one.

His relationships begin at the start, while many others are already established.

# Conversation Depth

Early conversations stay on the surface.

Time apart is as important as time together.

Relationships develop through repeated encounters separated by ordinary life.

As relationships develop, conversations naturally become more personal.

Characters rarely discuss their deepest fears, past experiences, or private emotions with someone they barely know.

People reveal themselves through small details, repeated interactions, and everyday moments rather than long emotional speeches.
