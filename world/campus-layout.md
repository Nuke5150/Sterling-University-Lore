# Sterling University Campus Layout

## Overview

Sterling University is a large, walkable campus where most academic buildings are within a ten to fifteen minute walk of one another.

Brick walkways, mature oak trees, landscaped courtyards, and open green spaces connect the major parts of campus. Students typically travel on foot, by bicycle, or electric scooter.

The campus feels busy during the day but becomes noticeably quieter in the evenings outside of residence halls and Greek Row.

---

# Campus Districts

The university naturally divides into several areas:

- Academic Core
- Student Life District
- Greek Row
- Residential Village
- Athletic Complex
- North Quad

Students often refer to these districts rather than giving full building names.

---

# Academic Core

The center of campus.

Contains most classroom buildings, faculty offices, lecture halls, and study spaces.

Students spend most weekdays moving between these buildings.

Major locations include:

- Business Building
- Science Center
- Humanities Hall
- University Library

Large brick pathways connect these buildings through shaded courtyards.

---

# Student Life District

The busiest part of campus outside class hours.

Students gather here to eat, study, meet friends, or simply pass through between classes.

Contains:

- Student Union
- Campus Bookstore
- Campus Coffee Kiosk
- Student Services
- Outdoor seating areas

This area is almost always active.

---

# University Library

Located between the Academic Core and the Student Life District.

Students often stop here before or after class.

The library serves as a transition between busy campus life and quiet study.

---

# North Quad

A large grassy courtyard surrounded by academic buildings.

Students study here on warm afternoons.

Campus organizations often set up information tables.

Many students cut across the Quad instead of using sidewalks.

The Quad serves as a common meeting place.

---

# Residential Village

Located on the quieter eastern side of campus.

Contains:

- Freshman residence halls
- Upperclassman residence halls
- Apartment-style housing
- Laundry facilities
- Recreation lounges

Students frequently gather here during evenings.

---

# Greek Row

Located just beyond the Residential Village.

Contains fraternity and sorority houses.

During the day it appears relatively quiet.

Evenings often bring social gatherings, philanthropy events, chapter meetings, and weekend parties.

Students not involved in Greek life still regularly walk through this area.

---

# Athletic Complex

Located on the western edge of campus.

Contains:

- Football stadium
- Recreation Center
- Flex Fitness Center
- Tennis courts
- Intramural fields

Students visit throughout the day for workouts, club sports, and athletic events.

---

# Parking

Most commuter parking sits around the outer edges of campus.

Students usually walk several minutes from parking lots to their classes.

Finding parking becomes difficult during mid-morning.

---

# Common Walking Routes

Students naturally follow several well-traveled paths:

Residence Halls → Student Union → Academic Core

Business Building ↔ Library

Student Union ↔ Library

North Quad ↔ Business Building

Greek Row ↔ Student Union

Residence Halls ↔ Recreation Center

These paths remain busy between classes.

---

# Time Between Locations

Approximate walking times:

2 minutes

3 minutes

2 minutes

5 minutes

6 minutes

Most destinations can be reached in under fifteen minutes.

---

# Daily Rhythm

Early Morning

Quiet campus.
Coffee lines begin forming.
Students walk with headphones and backpacks.

Late Morning

Heavy foot traffic.
Crowded sidewalks.
Classes changing every hour.

Lunch

Student Union becomes crowded.
Outdoor seating fills quickly.
Study groups begin forming.

Afternoon

Library grows busier.
Group projects meet in academic buildings.
Students move between classes.

Evening

Academic buildings empty.
Residence halls become active.
Greek Row begins hosting chapter meetings and social events.

Late Night

Library remains moderately busy.
Residence halls stay active.
Campus becomes calm except near Greek Row during weekends.

---

# Campus Personality

Sterling University feels established, lived-in, and academically respected.

Most students know familiar faces even if they are not friends.

Students develop routines:

The same coffee stop.

The same study table.

The same walking path.

The same classmates.

# Story Guidance

Characters naturally move through campus rather than remaining in one location.

Classes end.

Students walk together.

People pass one another between buildings.

Chance encounters often happen because students share the same daily routines rather than coincidence.

The campus should always feel active, even when the story focuses on only one conversation.

Because Blake transferred mid-semester, he enters a campus where these routines already exist.

Learning the campus becomes part of learning the people.
